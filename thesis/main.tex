\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
General-purpose large language models such as ChatGPT answer medical questions fluently, but in a specialised field they can state unsupported facts, cannot show where an answer comes from, and know nothing about the case at hand. This thesis addresses the topic of developing a domain expert with such a model by building a hybrid multimodal lab assistant for peripheral blood smears. Two image-analysis stages supply case evidence: a YOLOv8s detector locates white cells, red cells and platelets (mAP@0.50 = 0.985 on TXL-PBC), and an EfficientNet-B0 classifier assigns eight white-cell types (98.60\,\% accuracy on a held-out PBC test set). Every classified cell carries a Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty estimate, which identifies misclassified cells with an AUROC of 0.97, and a Grad-CAM saliency map. The domain expert interprets these findings in two forms: a GPT-4o agent that retrieves passages from 1{,}049 hematology textbook chunks (retrieval-augmented generation) and uses tools for reference ranges, cell counts and uncertainty; and an open-weight Llama-3.1-8B model fine-tuned with QLoRA on 1{,}890 textbook-grounded question--answer pairs. Fine-tuning made the open model's answers much closer to textbook reference answers (ROUGE-L 0.161 $\rightarrow$ 0.433) and stopped it from inventing book citations, but did not improve its clinical correctness or its accuracy on external exam questions; GPT-4o remained the most correct. Deterministic rules, not the language model, decide when a human expert must review a case; a real failure, in which the assistant over-interpreted a two-cell differential, led to an additional minimum-cell rule. The results suggest a layered design for LLM domain experts: fine-tuning for behaviour, retrieval for knowledge, tools for case data, and explicit rules for safety.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} large language models, domain expert, retrieval-augmented generation, QLoRA, blood smear, YOLOv8, EfficientNet, uncertainty estimation, clinical decision support
